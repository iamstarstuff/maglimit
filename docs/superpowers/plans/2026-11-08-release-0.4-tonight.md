# maglimit 0.4 (tonight) Implementation Plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** Answer "what's worth pointing my telescope at tonight, from here?" with a ranked list from `tonight()` and `maglimit tonight`, accounting for darkness, altitude, extinction and the Moon.

**Architecture:** A new `ephemeris.py` uses astropy once per night for the Sun, the Moon and sidereal time, then computes object altitudes with plain numpy for the whole catalogue at once. Three pure functions join `visibility.py`: airmass, extinction and a simple moonlit-sky brightness. `planner.py` gains `tonight()`, which runs the existing 0.3 model per object at its best time and ranks the results into an astropy `QTable`. `explain()` keeps its 0.3 answers.

**Tech Stack:** Python ≥3.10, numpy, astropy (already dependencies), `zoneinfo`, argparse, pytest, uv. One new dependency: `tzdata`, only on Windows.

**Spec:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-31-observing-planner-design.md` (§4 CLI and Python, §5.1 inputs, §5.3 ephemeris, §5.5 ranking, §8 phase 0.4, §9 property tests and performance).

## Global Constraints

- Don't break the v0.1–v0.3 API. `test/test_limiting_mag.py` and `test/test_golden.py` must pass **unchanged**, and `explain()` gives the same answers as 0.3.
- `visibility.py` stays pure: plain numbers in, plain numbers out, `math` only (spec §7, for the TypeScript port).
- Offline: nothing added may touch the network. astropy must never try to download IERS data (spec §5.3).
- Verdicts stay graded: `easy`, `moderate`, `challenging`, `very hard`, `not visible`.
- Spec §5.3 values: darkness = Sun below −18° (configurable), samples every 10 min, minimum altitude 20° (configurable), extinction k = 0.25 mag/airmass.
- Spec §5.5: `goal="easy"` is the default; `goal="challenge"` is the option.
- Spec §8 done-when: `maglimit tonight ...` ranks a believable list in under 2 s.
- Match existing style: Google-style docstrings, one docstring per test. `uv run ruff check .` must pass.

## Decisions made in this plan (differ from or add to the spec)

1. **Object altitudes come from numpy, not astropy `AltAz`.** astropy gives the local sidereal time and the Sun and Moon positions per sample. Object altitude is `sin(alt) = sin φ sin δ + cos φ cos δ cos(LST − α)`, vectorised over the catalogue. It uses OpenNGC's J2000 coordinates without precession, which is about 0.2° off in 2026 (checked against astropy for M31 at Pune: 67.22° vs 67.06°). That's well inside what matters here, and it ports directly to TypeScript.
2. **Extinction is `k·(X − 1)`, not `k·X`.** It's the extra dimming compared with the zenith. The 0.3 model and its golden cases are calibrated at the zenith, so an object overhead gets the same answer from `tonight()` and `explain()`. Airmass uses Kasten & Young (1989): `X = 1 / (sin h + 0.50572·(h + 6.07995)^−1.6364)`, with h in degrees.
3. **Moon penalty (simple, as decided).** When the Moon is above the horizon, it adds sky brightness `μ_moon = 18.0 − 2.5·log10(k) + 2.0·min(s, 90)/90` mag/arcsec², where k is the illuminated fraction and s the separation in degrees. This is added to the site's sky as flux. Full Moon at 90° turns a Bortle 3 sky (21.7) into 19.79, at 30° into 18.60. A crescent (k = 0.1) at 60° gives 21.01. The brightened sky goes through the same Crumey model, so verdicts stay consistent. Illumination comes from the Sun–Moon elongation e: `k = (1 − cos e)/2`. Krisciunas & Schaefer (1991) replaces this later (spec §8 "later").
4. **Best time.** Each object is assessed at two times: when it is highest in the dark window, and when it is highest while the Moon is down (if that time exists and the object is above the minimum altitude then). The better margin wins, so the list can say "03:10" after moonset.
5. **Ranking (§5.5).** Only objects with margin ≥ 0 are listed.
   - `goal="easy"`: score = margin + 0.5 if Messier + 0.01·(altitude − min_altitude), highest first.
   - `goal="challenge"`: lowest margin first (the hardest you can still see), then higher altitude.
   - Caldwell and Herschel 400 membership isn't in the bundled catalogue's fields, so 0.4 boosts Messier objects only.
6. **Hidden objects.** Objects above the minimum altitude with margin < 0 are counted in `plan.meta["hidden"]`. `show_hidden=True` (`--show-hidden`) appends them after the ranked list. Objects that never reach the minimum altitude are neither listed nor counted.
7. **Result type.** `tonight()` returns an astropy `QTable` (spec §4). Its columns and `meta` keys are listed in Task 3. A stable `to_dict()`/JSON shape is 0.5's job; 0.4's `--json` prints the rows as plain dicts.
8. **Dates and time zones (closes the §4 decide).** `date` is the local date the night *starts* on. With no date, it's tonight, but before local noon it's the night that is still running, so `maglimit tonight` at 02:00 shows the current night. The time zone is the system's, overridden by `tz=` / `--tz` with an IANA name. Times print as local HH:MM. `tzdata` is added for Windows, which has no system time-zone database.
9. **No darkness** (e.g. London in late June at −18°) raises `ValueError` naming the date and latitude and suggesting `sun_altitude=-12`. A night that is dark the whole time (polar night) works normally.
10. **Type filter.** `types=` takes friendly groups: `galaxy` = G, GPair, GTrpl, GGroup; `globular` = GCl; `open` = OCl, *Ass; `nebula` = HII, Neb, EmN, RfN, SNR, Cl+N; `pn` = PN. Anything else raises `ValueError` listing the groups. Dark nebulae (`DrkN`) and objects with no magnitude are never listed.
11. **Moon in the reasons.** When the Moon brightens an object's sky by 0.5 mag or more, the row's confidence is `low` with the reason "the Moon brightens the sky (simple estimate)".

## Review Focus

1. Running after midnight with no date: the plan is for the night still running, not the next evening. Pinned in Task 2 (`test_tonight_date_after_midnight`).
2. Summer at high latitude with no astronomical darkness: a clear error suggesting a shallower Sun altitude, never an empty table or a traceback. Pinned in Tasks 2 and 4.
3. No network: astropy must not try to download IERS tables, even for dates past its bundled data. Pinned in Task 2 (`test_night_never_downloads`).
4. Southern sites and objects that never rise (NGC 2070 from Pune peaks at about 2.5°): not listed and not counted as hidden. Pinned in Task 3.
5. A bright Moon next to the object never makes it easier to see, and a higher object never looks worse. Pinned as property tests in Tasks 1 and 3.

### Task 2: Ephemeris

**Files:**
- Create: `maglimit/ephemeris.py`, `test/test_ephemeris.py`
- Modify: `pyproject.toml` (add `"tzdata; sys_platform == 'win32'"` to dependencies), `uv.lock` (`uv lock`)

**Interfaces:**
- Produces:
  - `offline_iers()`: a context manager setting astropy's `iers.conf` `auto_download=False`, `auto_max_age=None` and `iers_degraded_accuracy="ignore"` with `set_temp`, so maglimit never changes the user's global astropy configuration.
  - `tonight_date(now: datetime) -> date`: `now.date()`, or the day before when `now.hour < 12`.
  - `altitudes(ra, dec, lat, lst) -> numpy.ndarray`: ra, dec are arrays in degrees, lat in degrees, lst an array of local sidereal times in degrees. Returns shape `(len(ra), len(lst))` in degrees (Decision 1).
  - `separations(ra1, dec1, ra2, dec2) -> numpy.ndarray`: angular separation in degrees, broadcasting like numpy (haversine form).
  - `Night`: frozen dataclass with `times` (astropy `Time` of the dark samples), `dark_start` and `dark_end` (aware `datetime` in the requested zone), `lst` (degrees, per sample), `moon_ra`, `moon_dec`, `moon_altitude`, `moon_illumination` (numpy arrays, per sample), `tz` (any `tzinfo`).
  - `night(site, date, tz, sun_altitude=-18.0, step_minutes=10) -> Night`: samples local noon on `date` to noon the next day every `step_minutes`, keeps the samples with the Sun below `sun_altitude`, and interpolates `dark_start`/`dark_end` linearly between the samples either side of the crossing (or uses the first/last sample when dark at noon). Uses `get_body("sun")`, `get_body("moon")` (builtin ephemeris), `AltAz` for Sun and Moon only, and `Time.sidereal_time("mean", longitude)`. All astropy calls run inside `offline_iers()`. Raises `ValueError` when `site.lat`/`site.lon` is missing, and `ValueError("no astronomical darkness on 2026-06-21 at latitude 51.5; try sun_altitude=-12")` (with the actual date and latitude) when no sample is dark.

**Interfaces:**
- Consumes: Task 1's `extinction`, `moon_sky_brightness`; Task 2's `night`, `altitudes`, `separations`, `tonight_date`, `Night`.
- Produces:
  - `_assess(obj, telescope, sqm, eye_pupil, field_factor, dimming=0.0) -> (margin, seen_as, magnification, eyepiece)`: the best over `_candidates`, with the object `dimming` magnitudes fainter (both its magnitude and its surface brightness). `explain()` is refactored to call it with `dimming=0.0`, and must return exactly what it did in 0.3.
  - `TYPE_GROUPS`: dict from Decision 10.
  - `tonight(telescope, site, date=None, tz=None, types=None, goal="easy", limit=20, min_altitude=20.0, sun_altitude=-18.0, show_hidden=False, eye_pupil=7.0, field_factor=2.0) -> QTable`. `date` is a `datetime.date` or an ISO string; `tz` a `ZoneInfo` or an IANA string, default the system zone (`datetime.now().astimezone().tzinfo`, used through `ZoneInfo` when it has a key, otherwise as-is). `goal` other than `easy`/`challenge`, an unknown type group, `limit < 1` or a bad date raise `ValueError`.
  - Columns, in order: `name` (`M31` for Messier objects, else the catalogue name), `catalogue_name`, `type`, `magnitude`, `major_axis`, `minor_axis` (NaN when unknown), `best_time` (`Time`), `altitude` (deg Quantity), `magnification`, `eyepiece` (NaN when none), `margin`, `verdict`, `seen_as`, `moon_separation` (deg Quantity, NaN when the Moon is down at `best_time`), `confidence`, `reasons` (`"; "`-joined string). Floats rounded to 2 decimals like `explain()`.
  - `meta`: `date` (ISO string), `tz` (string), `dark_start`, `dark_end` (aware datetimes), `sky_sqm`, `moon_illumination` (at the middle dark sample, rounded to 2), `star_limit` (`telescope.sky_lim_mag(site, eye_pupil=eye_pupil)`), `goal`, `hidden` (int).

Per object (vectorised where it's cheap): highest altitude over the dark samples and its sample; highest altitude over moon-down samples (`moon_altitude <= 0`) and its sample. For each candidate sample whose altitude ≥ `min_altitude`: sky = `moon_sky_brightness(site.sqm, illum, separation, moon_alt)` at that sample, `dimming = extinction(alt)`, then `_assess`. Keep the better margin (Decision 4). Reasons are `_reasons(obj, sky)` plus the Moon reason (Decision 11). Rank and cut per Decisions 5 and 6.
